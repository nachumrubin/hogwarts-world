# Tree Lab — Phase 4.2: LOD visual consistency

Baseline: e1a69a6 (Phase 4.1). Skeletons, bark, crown architecture, species identities and the LOD0 foliage
distribution are frozen and unchanged: LOD0 foliage instances are generated by unchanged code (same counts and
positions), and leafless renders are unchanged. Everything in this phase is in the lower-detail representations,
their atlases, the transition logic and the measurement tools. No wind, seasons, species or world integration.

LOD0 is the reference. Every number below compares a lower LOD with the next finer one **at the switch**: both
sides rendered from the same camera, at the projected size where the switch happens, in daylight on the neutral
plot. Tools: `tools/evidence/lodcheck.js` (renders) and `tools/lod_metrics.py` (metrics); sheets and tables in
`review/lod/`.

Metrics:

* **Crown coverage:** silhouette pixels over the finer side's crown envelope (its silhouette closed with a disk of
  3.5 % of the image, holes filled), in percent; the difference is in percentage points.
* **Silhouette overlap:** intersection over union of the two silhouettes.
* **Mean foliage luminance:** Rec. 709 luma of the mean linear colour of foliage pixels (silhouette pixels that
  are green-dominant); difference in percent. **ΔE:** CIE76 difference of the two mean foliage colours.
* **Triangles, instance memory** of the tree at each side; **overdraw:** foliage fragments passing the alpha test
  per covered pixel (no depth rejection).

## 1. What was wrong (diagnosis)

**LOD1 darker and flatter.** Measured on Oak B at the switch, LOD1 was 41–45 % darker than LOD0 (foliage
luminance). In order of size:

1. **Leaf undersides.** LOD0 draws a leaf's underside paler (×1.45, greyer) whenever its back faces the viewer:
   looking up into a crown most visible leaves are undersides. The card atlas had leaves drawn from one side,
   with a 2D normal map, so a card seen from below showed upper sides, mirrored. Alone this was most of the
   difference under overcast light (−27 %).
2. **Leaf size and density.** The atlas sprays did not match the generator: the pine's card leader was 2.4 × the
   generator's mean shoot (so its needles were drawn 2.4 × too small), the birch's 1.4 ×; the oak sprays carried
   1.6 × the generator's leaves per metre. Coverage at the switch was off by up to 38 points.
3. **Coverage-preserving mips per layer.** One alpha scale per mip level for a whole atlas layer is set by its
   densest cell (the clump card), and sparse cells (a birch spray) dissolved with distance (birch LOD1 coverage
   22 % against LOD0's 62 %).
4. **Normals and lighting.** Cards were lit by a flat "volume" approximation (0.45 + 0.35 N·L), with the second
   card of each pair turned sideways, away from the sky the leaves face.
5. **Twigs drawn into the cards** (every lateral) darkened the minified foliage.

**LOD2 streaks.** The vertical streaks in Oak B's Phase 4.1 transition sheet came from the clump cards'
construction: upright cards turned edge-on to the camera became one-pixel-wide vertical slivers, and the clump
texture was a dome of 2D shoot sprites with no depth. The sheet that went out with 4.1 was also stale: it had been
composed before the tilted-card ("tent") fix, which I hadn't re-rendered into the sheet. The tent clumps removed
the slivers but stayed inconsistent from above (+22–38 % luminance, ±16 points of coverage).

**Impostors brighter and yellower.** Two defects, found by rendering the impostors' own albedo and normals beside
LOD1's (`extraInBake` diagnostic):

1. The bake stored the fragment's (fractional) alpha, while alpha-to-coverage turned that same alpha into fewer
   covered samples. Dividing the resolved colour by the resolved alpha therefore brightened the albedo by up to
   1.5 × and bent the octahedral normals (mean normal y −0.10 instead of +0.30).
2. The run-time shading was an approximation of the card shading, not the leaf shading.

## 2. Corrections

**Spray cards, rebuilt from the generator (`tools/foliage_textures.py`).** Each spray is now generated in 3D with the
generator's own rules (leader → laterals → sub-laterals; leaf attitudes term for term from `framework/foliage.ts`;
light towards the card's front, gravity behind it) and projected onto the card:

* each leaf is pasted with a full 3D affine projection (foreshortened when tilted, skipped when edge-on), painted
  back to front;
* the atlas stores, per texel, the leaf's **upper-side normal in card space**, perturbed by the scan's normal map
  exactly as the renderer does (normalize(N + 0.8 · map)), and, in the translucency channel, which side of the leaf
  faces the card front (mip-averaged into a share). At run time the shader works out per texel whether the viewer
  sees upper sides or undersides, pales undersides exactly as LOD0 does, turns the normal to the viewer, and lights
  the texel with LOD0's leaf shading, term for term (no card-specific factors left);
* leader lengths match the generator's mean shoot per species (oak 0.62 m, birch 0.25 m, pine 0.084 m), so leaves
  keep their true size when a card is scaled to its spray;
* leaf density per card is the generator's (sun factor included) times a per-species factor that sets crown
  coverage at the switch (oak 0.85, birch 1.05, pine 1.0; a spray is two cards);
* only the leader's wood is drawn.

**Cards turned to the sky.** A spray's two cards form a V about its axis, each 40° from world up made perpendicular to
the axis: the atlas leaves lean to the card's front, as LOD0's leaves lean to the light.

**Coverage-preserving mips per atlas cell** (sprites 4 × 2, cards 2 × 2), applied in the copy of the mip chain.
Pine cards take the needles' alpha boost, as their content is needles.

**LOD2: evaluated and dropped from the distance chain (section 3).** Its clump atlas was rebuilt the same way (a
cluster of generated sprays seen from the side, plus a second view from above for the cap card), which brought it
closer, but it was still the least consistent level.

**Impostors.**

* The bake stores coverage that matches its alpha (alpha 0 or 1 per sample), so dividing by it is exact.
* The bake source is LOD1, now matched to LOD0. A LOD0 bake was evaluated on the birch (section 4): 23 % darker than
  the geometry from the side, and 5.8 × the bake time.
* Run-time shading is LOD0's leaf shading applied to the atlas's albedo and normals. Two things are particular to
  the impostor: the frames are view directions, so the visible side of each leaf (and its paler underside) is
  already in the albedo; and the crown's shadow on itself, which the geometry gets from the shadow map, is
  approximated from the baked sky exposure (sun × exposure^0.6, the exponent measured against LOD1). Bark and
  twigs are told apart from leaves in the normal atlas, so only leaves transmit.
* The same mip-aware alpha as the leaves; a silhouette mode (for the measurements); fog only at run time; nothing
  of the light baked in.

## 4. Impostor evaluation (§4)

* **Bake source:** birch, four cameras (eye, between frames, elevated, overhead), LOD0 geometry as reference.
  Baked from LOD0: −23 % luminance from the side, bake 29 min on SwiftShader. Baked from LOD1: −3 % to +5 % from the
  side before the bake-alpha fix (and see the table for after it), 5 min. LOD0's sub-pixel leaves and thin hanging
  shoots do not survive 112 px frames as coverage; LOD1 is already a minified representation of them. Chosen:
  LOD1.
* **Atlas resolution:** 112 px frames, 9 × 9 (1008² per species, 51.7 MiB for five). At the 140 px switch the crown
  covers about 90 px of a frame, so texels and pixels are within 1.25 × of each other. 160 px frames would need
  ~105 MiB for no gain at the switch size.
* **Frame interpolation:** three frames per pixel, barycentric weights, each sampled along the view ray: no visible
  rotation (`review/crown/impostor-sweep-*.jpg` from 4.1 still applies; the videos below show it in motion).
* **Depth-aware reprojection:** not implemented. It needs a depth atlas (+50 % memory, or a channel the atlases do
  not have free) and its benefit is parallax inside a crown between frames 11° apart. At ≤ 140 px the measured
  silhouette overlap with LOD1 is already 0.52–0.88.
* **Normal-based relighting, colour consistency:** section 2; measured in section 5.
* **Shadow consistency:** impostors cast shadows from the frames towards the sun and receive other trees' shadows;
  their own crown's shadow is the exposure term.
* **Thin branches and pendulous birch foliage:** the hanging curtains survive as coverage in the frames (birch
  coverage +2…+4 points at the switch); single thin branches below a texel do not, as at that size in LOD1.
* **Overhead and steep angles:** the zenith is an exact frame; overhead luminance is within 0–7 % of LOD1 after the
  fixes (it was +48–55 %).

## 6. Transitions (§5)

* **Hysteresis:** a tree moves to finer detail only when its projected height is 12 % past the threshold, and to
  coarser detail only 12 % below it. A tree standing at a threshold, or a camera orbiting at constant distance, does
  not flip.
* **Temporal blending:** a LOD change cross-fades over 0.6 s. The outgoing and incoming representations are drawn
  with complementary 4 × 4 ordered-dither patterns (each pixel belongs to exactly one of them, in the bark, the
  foliage and the impostor shaders alike), so a tree is never doubled or holed while it fades. The shadow is cast
  by whichever side has more weight.
* The fade only hides the step that remains: section 5 shows the representations themselves agree before fading.
* Stand mode applies this live (every frame while trees fade, otherwise when the camera moves). Scripts and the
  benchmark pin the stand's camera.

**Recordings** (`review/lod/lod-ground.mp4`, `lod-aerial.mp4`; 480 × 270, 5 and 4 fps, LOD chosen as on a 1080p screen, real-time camera paths, frames
rendered offscreen with the same LOD machinery and labelled with the trees per LOD and when a cross-fade runs):

* ground (12-tree stand): walking towards Oak B across its LOD0/LOD1 switch (≈ 36 m) at 2.3 m/s and back; orbiting
  it at 45 m; moving out through the stand (up to 60 m/s) and back in, several trees switching together;
* aerial (12-tree stand, 4 fps): vertical ascent from 15 m to 120 m; broom flight at 60 m and 30 m/s; a pass directly
  over the canopy at 35 m (27 m/s); descent to 15 m. The 50-tree stand was tried: with the chosen chain nearly all
  50 trees stay at LOD1 from that height on a 1080p screen, and SwiftShader took ~4 minutes a frame.

Ground recording: Oak B goes to LOD0 as the camera closes in (frames 30–32 cross-fade) and back to LOD1 walking away
(frames 50–52); the orbit at constant distance changes nothing (hysteresis); moving out and back through the stand,
trees cross LOD0 ↔ LOD1 and LOD1 ↔ impostor in groups (frames 84–119; at most 3 impostors at the 1080p thresholds), each a 3-frame dithered fade. Per-frame LOD counts are
burned into the video and in `review/lod/video-*-frames.json`.

Aerial recording: over the canopy at 35 m the trees below cross LOD1 ↔ LOD0 (frames 54–64, up to three at once), and

## 8. Deficiencies

* The boundary targets are met in 7 of 20 measurements for coverage and 12 of 20 for luminance (section 5). Worst
  cases: the pine's LOD0 → LOD1 colour (−21 % from the side, −40 % from above); LOD1's coverage from overhead for the
  oaks and the pine (−9…−11 points); the oak impostors' coverage (+7…+12 points).
* The impostor shows dark contour lines inside its blobs when magnified (≥ 2 × the switch size): blended octahedral
  normals across leaf/gap edges. At the switch size they are below a pixel or two.
* The cross-fade casts the shadow from one side only and switches it at mid-fade: a faint shadow change.
* Ground-level views now keep LOD1 down to 140 px, so they carry more triangles than chain A (section 3);
  untested on hardware.
* The LOD2 clump cards remain in the data and in the LOD menu but are not in the distance chain.
* Recordings are 5 (ground) and 4 (aerial) frames per second: each frame is rendered offscreen by SwiftShader (about a minute a frame), so
  real-time playback is not possible in this environment. The camera paths are real time; the frames are spaced
  0.2 s apart, which is enough to see each cross-fade (0.6 s, 3 frames) but not fine motion.
* No hardware timings.
